From Drone Pixels to Crop Yield

Follow the step-by-step journey of how a Convolutional Neural Network transforms digital camera pixels into a single harvest yield prediction (bu/ac).

Step 1

The Image as Numbers: Decimals Between 0.0 and 1.0

Why the computer divides RGB (0–255) by 255 and standardizes them
The Core Rule: A computer cannot see corn leaves or soil. It only sees a grid of numbers. If we leave numbers between 0 and 255, the math numbers become too huge and cause math overflow. Dividing by 255.0 scales everything between 0.0 (pitch dark) and 1.0 (brightest color).

Step 2

The Convolution: Sliding Pattern Detectors (3×3 Filters)

How the computer multiplies small patches of pixels by pattern weights and sums them up
The Core Rule: The CNN takes a small $3 \times 3$ grid of numbers called a Filter (or Kernel). It slides this filter over every $3 \times 3$ window of pixels, multiplies matching pairs, and adds them up. If the pattern matches, the sum is a big positive number!

Step 3

The Activation Gate: Filtering Out Noise (ReLU / Swish)

Why negative numbers turn into zero to keep only meaningful biological signals
The Core Rule: Real nature is non-linear. If a patch has bare dirt or confusing shadows, the convolution result might be negative (e.g. $-1.8$). We don't want negative leaves! The ReLU function ($\max(0, x)$) and Swish function ($x \cdot \sigma(x)$) automatically wipe negative noise to $0$, passing only strong positive signals forward.

Step 4

Downsampling & Pooling: Shrinking Space to Gain Perspective

How the computer reduces a 4×4 grid of details into a 2×2 grid of summary concepts
The Core Rule: You don't need to track every millimeter of leaf vein to know if a corn field is healthy. Max Pooling looks at a $2 \times 2$ block of 4 numbers and keeps only the highest number. This cuts the size in half while keeping the most critical clues.

Step 5

The Deep Pyramid: From Edges to Full Corn Plants

How EfficientNet-B0 stacks 16 layers, shrinking 2D space while expanding conceptual depth
The Core Rule: Early layers see simple visual edges. As the data flows deeper into the network, spatial dimensions shrink from $224 \times 224$ down to $7 \times 7$, but the number of channels grows from $3$ up to $1,280$. The CNN assembles simple lines into leaves, leaves into crop rows, and crop rows into flowering tassels.

Step 6

Global Average Pooling: Collapsing 2D Space into 1,280 Numbers

How the 7×7 grid is averaged out, leaving a pure 1D list of visual clues
The Core Rule: We no longer care where inside the patch a corn plant is located (left or right); we only care how much total healthy corn exists. Global Average Pooling averages all 49 numbers in each $7 \times 7$ feature slice, turning $(7 \times 7 \times 1,280)$ into a flat 1D vector of exactly 1,280 numbers.

Step 7

The Scorecard: Turning 1,280 Clues into Final Harvest Yield

How learned weights multiply each clue to output one continuous harvest number (bu/ac)
The Core Rule: The final Linear Regression layer multiplies each of the $1,280$ visual clues by its own learned weight $w_i$. High greenness and full flowering tassels add bonus yield (+), while yellow dying leaves and bare ground subtract penalties (−). Everything adds up to the final crop yield!

The Complete Journey in One Sentence

A CNN divides raw image pixels by 255 to create decimals between 0 and 1, slides 3×3 pattern filters to find leaf edges and row structures, filters out negative noise with ReLU, shrinks the spatial resolution via pooling, and aggregates 1,280 biological traits to calculate the final harvest yield.
