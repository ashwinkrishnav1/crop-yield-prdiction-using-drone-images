Layer 1

Layer 1: The CNN Creates 32 Pattern-Detecting Filters

Where do the 3×3 filter numbers come from? They start as RANDOM GUESSES and learn during training!
Key Concept — Where Do the Filter Numbers Come From?
When the CNN is first created (before any training), the 3×3 filters are filled with completely random numbers like 0.03, -0.12, 0.45. They have no idea what a corn leaf looks like!

During training, the CNN is shown thousands of corn images WITH known harvest yields. Each time it guesses wrong, the math (called Backpropagation) nudges the filter numbers slightly in the right direction. After seeing thousands of images, the random filters have automatically evolved into expert edge detectors, color detectors, and texture detectors!

You never program what the filters should look for. The CNN learns it on its own!

Activation

The Activation Gate: Killing Negative Noise, Keeping Real Signals

Every number on every Feature Map passes through this rule IMMEDIATELY after convolution
Why do we need this?
When the filter slides over flat dirt or confusing shadows, the math might give a negative number (e.g., −1.8). But a negative leaf edge makes no sense! The activation function simply says: "If the number is negative → turn it into 0. If it's positive → keep it."

This is called ReLU (Rectified Linear Unit): output = max(0, input)
EfficientNet-B0 uses a smoother version called Swish: output = x × sigmoid(x)

Shrinking

Downsampling: Cutting the Size in Half to Speed Things Up

Before Layer 2 starts, the CNN shrinks each 222×222 map down to 112×112
Why shrink? A 222×222 grid has ~49,000 numbers per feature map. With 32 maps, that's 1.6 MILLION numbers! The computer would be extremely slow processing all of them. Max Pooling looks at every 2×2 block (4 numbers) and keeps only the biggest one (the strongest signal). This cuts the size in half while preserving the important clues!

Layer 2+

Layer 2 and Beyond: Combining Simple Clues into Complex Biological Features

This is where the CNN becomes truly smart — it combines edges into leaves, and leaves into tassels!
The Magic of Deep Layers:
Layer 1 looked at raw pixels and found simple lines/edges. But a line is not a corn leaf! In Layer 2, the filters look at ALL 32 Feature Maps from Layer 1 at the same time. They combine clues:

• Horizontal edge (Map #1) + Vertical edge (Map #2) + High greenness (Map #3) → "This is a corn leaf corner!"
• No edges anywhere (Maps #1–5 are all zero) + Low greenness (Map #3 is zero) → "This is bare soil, skip it."

Layer 2 creates 64 new Feature Maps (more sheets of numbers). Each new sheet represents a more complex idea!

How Layer 2 Filters Are Different From Layer 1:

Layer 1 Filter (Simple)
• Looks at: 3 input channels (Red, Green, Blue)
• Size: 3×3×3 = 27 weights
• Finds: Basic edges, lines, color blobs
• Output: 32 feature maps
Layer 2 Filter (Smarter)
• Looks at: 32 input channels (all of Layer 1's maps!)
• Size: 3×3×32 = 288 weights
• Finds: Leaf shapes, canopy patches, row corners
• Output: 64 feature maps
Key Insight: Each deeper layer reads ALL the maps from the previous layer. So Layer 3 reads 64 maps, Layer 4 reads 128 maps. The filters get bigger in depth (more channels to read) even though they stay 3×3 in spatial size. This is how the CNN builds increasingly complex understanding!

What Each Layer Learns to See (Click to Expand):

Layers 1–2: Lines, Edges, and Simple Color Contrasts
112 × 112 × 32
🔹 What it sees: Straight lines (horizontal crop rows, vertical leaf blades), edges between green and brown, color intensity differences.
🔹 Real farm example: "There's a sharp line at pixel row 45 where bright green suddenly changes to dark brown = crop row edge."
🔹 Agricultural value: Tells the CNN where the plants are versus where the soil is.
Layers 3–4: Leaf Textures, Canopy Overlap, and Gap Patterns
56 × 56 × 64
🔹 What it sees: Repeated leaf textures, overlapping leaf canopy areas, inter-row gaps, patches of consistent green vs brown.
🔹 Real farm example: "This 56×56 region has dense overlapping leaves = thick canopy" vs "This region has gaps between rows = sparse stand."
🔹 Agricultural value: Measures canopy closure — how much of the soil is covered by leaves. More canopy = more photosynthesis = more yield!
Layers 5–6: Flowering Tassels, Ear Shoots, and Drought Symptoms
14 × 14 × 192
🔹 What it sees: Complete biological structures: yellow tassel clusters at the top of corn plants, curling leaves (drought stress), missing plants (empty spots in a row).
🔹 Real farm example: "This 14×14 zone shows feathery yellow-white tassel structures = the plant is at flowering stage (R1)" or "Brown curling leaves = the plant is under severe heat stress."
🔹 Agricultural value: Directly relates to yield! Good tassels = good pollination = full ears of corn. Drought stress = fewer kernels.
Final Block (Layer 16): Complete Agronomic Summary of the Plot
7 × 7 × 1,280
🔹 What it sees: The entire biological state of the corn plot compressed into 1,280 conceptual dimensions.
🔹 Each of the 1,280 channels represents a different high-level trait: chlorophyll health, tassel density, plant population, drought severity, weed pressure, canopy uniformity, etc.
🔹 The 7×7 grid means only 49 spatial positions remain from the original 224×224. Each position covers roughly a 32×32 pixel area of the original image.
🔹 Agricultural value: This IS the "digital health report" of the crop. It contains everything the CNN learned from the image!

Global Average Pooling: 7×7 Grid → 1 Number Per Channel = 1,280 Clue Numbers

The CNN no longer cares WHERE inside the patch something is — only HOW MUCH of it exists
Why average? We don't care if the flowering tassel is in the top-left or bottom-right corner of the patch. We only care: How strong is the tassel signal overall? By averaging the 49 numbers (7×7) in each sheet, we collapse all spatial info into one single score per trait.

Final Step

The Final Equation: 1,280 Numbers × Weights = One Harvest Yield

The simplest math in the entire CNN: multiply each clue by its importance and add them all up!
The simplest step! Each of the 1,280 clue numbers is multiplied by a learned weight that says how important that clue is for predicting yield. Healthy green foliage gets a positive weight (+), meaning it adds yield. Drought stress gets a negative weight (−), meaning it reduces yield. Everything is summed up into one final number!

🌽 The Complete Journey in 60 Seconds

Before CNN: Drone image → pixels divided by 255 → decimals between 0 and 1 → standardized around 0.

Layer 1: 32 randomly-born 3×3 filters (trained to perfection) slide across every position, multiplying & adding → 32 Feature Maps of edge/color clues → Activation wipes negatives to 0.

Shrinking: Max Pooling halves the size (224→112) keeping only the strongest signals.

Layers 2–16: Repeat! Each new layer reads ALL previous maps, combining simple clues into complex biological structures (edges → leaves → tassels). Size shrinks (112→56→28→14→7), channels grow (32→64→128→320→1,280).

Collapse: Global Average Pooling averages each 7×7 sheet into 1 number → flat list of 1,280 clue numbers.

Final Math: Each clue × its learned weight → all summed → One single harvest yield: 164.8 bu/ac!
